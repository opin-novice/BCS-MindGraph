## Abstract

General Knowledge (GK) Multiple-Choice Question (MCQ) generation for high-stakes competitive examinations — such as the Bangladesh Civil Service (BCS) Preliminary examination — presents fundamental challenges rooted in temporal validity, hallucinated distractors, and absence of evidence provenance. Standard Large Language Models (LLMs) and untemporalized Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) systems frequently exhibit post-cutoff data leakage (t > 2023-04-19), generating options that were historically accurate but epistemically invalid at the declared exam cutoff. We propose **BCSBatighor-GK**, an end-to-end framework integrating: (1) a **Bitemporal Knowledge Graph (KG)** with dual valid-time/transaction-time timestamps, (2) a **Time-Slice Snapshot Query Engine** enforcing strict cutoff isolation, (3) a **Provenance-Tracked Episodic Web Retrieval Module**, (4) a **Temporally Contrastive Distractor Engine**, and (5) an automated **Rejection Taxonomy Quality Gate** (Section 10.2 Standard). We evaluate across three strong baselines (Generic LLM, Static RAG, Web-RAG) over 144 MCQs (36 questions x 4 systems). Results demonstrate that the Proposed System achieves **100% Temporal Correctness**, **97.22% Factual Validity**, and **94.44% Distractor Quality**, with all five research hypotheses (H1-H5) confirmed at p < 0.001. A blinded human evaluation by 3 BCS-cadre expert raters yields an overall Likert score of **4.81/5.0** with Krippendorff alpha = **0.9784**. A real holdout evaluation on 22 authentic 45th BCS exam questions yields **MRR = 0.6818**, transparently bounded by a seed corpus of N=69 facts.

**Keywords**: Bitemporal Knowledge Graph, MCQ Generation, Temporal Cutoff Enforcement, Retrieval-Augmented Generation, Rejection Taxonomy, Bangladesh Civil Service Examination, Low-Resource NLP.

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Motivation and Problem Statement

The Bangladesh Civil Service (BCS) examination is a high-stakes national-level competitive examination admitting approximately 2,000-3,000 officers annually across 27 cadres, from an applicant pool exceeding 400,000 candidates per cycle. The General Knowledge (GK) section of the BCS Preliminary examination tests mastery across 11 core topic domains, including Bangladesh Affairs, Liberation War, Constitutional Law, International Relations, and Information & Communication Technology. Preparing a reliable, temporally-valid, and pedagogically sound MCQ bank for this examination is an urgent practical challenge for the Bangladesh Public Service Commission (BPSC).

Automated MCQ generation via Large Language Models presents three distinct failure modes in this setting:

**Failure Mode 1: Temporal Cutoff Violations.** LLMs carry parametric factual memory that extends arbitrarily beyond any declared exam date. A question asking "Who is the current Prime Minister of Bangladesh?" generated by a standard LLM in 2023 may produce correct facts as of 2024 (post-exam), constituting a temporal leakage error. Our empirical evaluation confirms that the Generic LLM baseline produces a **38.89% temporal leakage rate** — i.e., 38.89% of generated MCQs contain at least one option valid only after t* = 2023-04-19.

**Failure Mode 2: Distractor Quality Degradation.** LLMs frequently generate distractors that are (a) trivially distinguishable from the correct answer, (b) grammatically inconsistent with the correct option, or (c) semantically synonymous with the correct answer (creating a multi-correct-option defect). The Generic LLM baseline achieves only **66.67% Distractor Quality** versus **94.44%** for our proposed system.

**Failure Mode 3: Missing Evidence Provenance.** Existing RAG systems retrieve document chunks without verifying their temporal validity. A static TF-IDF corpus built in 2024 and queried at t* = 2023-04-19 would retrieve facts valid in 2024 without distinguishing them from 2023-valid facts — a fundamental epistemic provenance failure.

### 1.2 Contributions

This paper makes the following primary contributions:

1. **BCSBatighor-GK Framework**: An end-to-end bitemporal MCQ generation system with six tightly-integrated architectural components.
2. **Bitemporal BCS Corpus**: A 494-question annotated dataset with mandatory temporal_class, topic, difficulty, reference_date, and supporting_fact_ids fields; temporal_class inter-annotator kappa = 0.8906 (Almost Perfect).
3. **Rejection Taxonomy (Section 10.2 Standard)**: A ten-code interpretable defect taxonomy enabling transparent, auditable MCQ quality screening.
4. **Statistically Rigorous Evaluation**: H1-H5 confirmed via chi-square tests (p < 0.001); three-variant component ablation study; blinded human evaluation (alpha = 0.9784).
5. **45th BCS Real Holdout Benchmark**: First evaluation of a MCQ generation system against authentic 45th BCS Preliminary questions under strict temporal cutoff (MRR = 0.6818).

## 2. Related Work

### 2.1 MCQ Generation via Neural Language Models

Early neural MCQ generation relied on template-based approaches [CITE] and question-answer pair extraction from structured knowledge bases. The advent of pre-trained LLMs (GPT, T5, BERT-based models) enabled end-to-end MCQ generation from unstructured text [CITE], though these approaches lack temporal grounding.

### 2.2 Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)

RAG systems [CITE: Lewis et al., 2020] augment LLM generation with retrieved document context, improving factual grounding. However, standard RAG systems operate over static indices without temporal filtering, making them unsuitable for time-sensitive exam settings where a strict cutoff must be enforced.

### 2.3 Temporal Knowledge Graphs

Bitemporal database theory [CITE: Snodgrass, 1992; Jensen et al., 1994] distinguishes valid time (when a fact holds in the real world) from transaction time (when a fact was recorded in the database). Recent work on temporal knowledge graphs (TKGs) has applied bitemporal modeling to event prediction [CITE] and question answering [CITE], but no prior work has applied bitemporality to MCQ generation for high-stakes examinations.

### 2.4 Exam-Specific MCQ Generation

Domain-specific MCQ generation for medical licensing [CITE], legal bar exams [CITE], and language proficiency exams [CITE] has demonstrated the value of domain-constrained generation. Our work is the first, to our knowledge, to address the BCS examination's unique temporal-cutoff requirements.

## 3. System Architecture: BCSBatighor-GK

### 3.1 Overview

BCSBatighor-GK comprises six modular components connected by a strict temporal guard at each interface boundary:

```
[Bitemporal KG (kg_builder.py)]
         |  Snapshot at t* = 2023-04-19
         v
[Time-Slice Snapshot Engine]
         |  Temporally-admissible facts only
         v
[Episodic Web Store (episodic_store.py)]
         |  Source-tier weighted, post-cutoff filtered
         v
[MCQ Generator (mcq_generator.py)]
         |  4 system variants; Challenger-Reasoner-Judge loop
         v
[Quality Gate Screener (mcq_quality.py)]
         |  10-code rejection taxonomy
         v
[Verified MCQ Output + Audit Trail]
```

### 3.2 Bitemporal Knowledge Graph

Every fact node Fi in the Knowledge Graph is stored as a bitemporal tuple:

    Fact(Fi) = < text, entities, relation, Vf, Vt, Sp, S_tier, status >

where:
- Vf: valid-from date (when this fact became true in the world)
- Vt: valid-to date (when this fact ceased to be true; inf if currently active)
- Sp: source publication date (when this evidence was published/retrieved)
- S_tier in {TIER_1_OFFICIAL, TIER_2_GOVT_NEWS, TIER_3_COMMERCIAL, TIER_4_UNVERIFIED}: source reliability class

**Temporal Admissibility Rule**: A fact Fi is admissible for MCQ generation at cutoff t* if and only if:

    Vf <= t*  AND  Vt >= t*  AND  Sp <= t*

Facts violating this inequality are automatically excluded from the generation context.

### 3.3 Time-Slice Snapshot Engine

The snapshot engine reconstructs the exact world-state at t* via:

    Snapshot(t*) = { Fi | (Vf <= t* <= Vt) AND (Sp <= t*) }

This eliminates post-cutoff facts (e.g., 2024 cabinet reshuffles) and pre-world facts (not yet valid at t*), providing a clean temporal context window for generation.

### 3.4 Provenance-Tracked Episodic Store

The episodic store maintains timestamped web retrieval logs for each evidence snippet. Source weight is computed as:

    w(Fi) = S_tier_weight * exp(-lambda * snapshot_age)

where snapshot_age = (t* - Sp) in days and lambda is a recency decay parameter. Evidence with Sp > t* is rejected via is_admissible_evidence() regardless of source tier.

### 3.5 Temporally Contrastive Distractor Engine

To create plausible, non-trivial distractors, we employ two strategies:

1. **Past-State Distractors**: Historical facts where Vt < t* (e.g., former office holders, previous statistical values) are used as plausible-yet-definitively-incorrect options.
2. **Semantic Confusers**: Graph neighborhood overlap and domain taxonomy clusters (DOMAIN_CONFUSER_TAXONOMY) provide semantically related but factually distinct entities, while SYNONYM_CLASSES explicitly excludes direct synonyms.

### 3.6 Rejection Taxonomy Quality Gate (Section 10.2 Standard)

All candidate MCQs undergo dual screening:

**Stage 1 - Rule-Based Screener (RuleBasedScreener)**: Detects structural defects:
- E-MULTI: Multiple valid/correct options at t*
- E-DUP: Near-duplicate of existing corpus question
- E-STYLE: Formatting, transliteration, or ASCII numeral errors

**Stage 2 - Factuality Verification Engine (FactualityVerificationEngine)**: Detects semantic defects:
- E-TIME: Temporal cutoff violation (fact valid only after t*)
- E-LEAK: Information leakage in question stem
- E-UNSUP: Unsupported claim / hallucination (no KG grounding)
- E-DIST: Implausible or trivially distinguishable distractor
- E-AMB: Ambiguous stem or option set
- E-KG: Missing bitemporal KG fact link
- E-SRC: Low-credibility source tier (TIER_4_UNVERIFIED)

## 4. Experimental Setup

### 4.1 Dataset: BCS Question Corpus

We curated a dataset of **494 verified historical BCS exam questions** spanning 11 General Knowledge topics from the 14th through 45th BCS examinations (1992-2023). Each question is annotated with:

- **topic** and **subtopic**: Domain classification
- **difficulty**: Ordinal {EASY, MEDIUM, HARD}
- **reference_date**: Temporal anchor for the correct answer
- **source_exam**: BCS cycle identifier
- **temporal_class**: One of {EVERGREEN, TEMPORAL_ANCHORED, MUTABLE_POST_CUTOFF}

**Temporal Class Distribution**:
- EVERGREEN: 427 questions (86.4%) — historically fixed facts (e.g., "In which year did Bangladesh gain independence?")
- TEMPORAL_ANCHORED: 47 questions (9.5%) — time-specific but non-changing (e.g., questions about Liberation War dates)
- MUTABLE_POST_CUTOFF: 20 questions (4.1%) — facts that may change with time (e.g., GDP rankings, population figures)

**Inter-Annotator Agreement (Section 5.4)**:

Agreement was measured between a BCS-cadre Subject Matter Expert (SME) and an independent text-based rule classifier across all 494 questions:

| Dimension | Cohen Kappa | Observed Agreement | Interpretation |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :--- |
| temporal_class | 0.8906 | 97.37% | Almost Perfect Agreement |
| topic | 0.6238 | 64.37% | Substantial Agreement |
| difficulty | 0.1575 | 56.07% | Fair/Slight Agreement |

The low difficulty kappa reflects genuine human subjectivity in perceiving question complexity — this divergence is expected and transparently reported without modification.

### 4.3 System Variants

1. **Generic LLM Baseline**: Direct parametric LLM prompting without any RAG or KG context.
2. **Static RAG Baseline**: TF-IDF keyword retrieval over an untemporalized static corpus.
3. **Web-RAG Baseline**: Provenance-tracked pre-cutoff web sentence retrieval (no timestamp filtering).
4. **Proposed Bitemporal KG System**: Full BCSBatighor-GK with snapshot engine, contrastive distractors, and quality gate screener.

## 5. Main Results

### 5.1 Comparative Benchmark Evaluation (N=144 MCQs, t*=2023-04-19)

| System | Factual Validity | Temporal Correctness | Exam Relevance | Distractor Quality | Rejection Rate |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| Generic LLM Baseline | 0.6111 | 0.6111 | 1.0000 | 0.6667 | 0.0000 |
| Static RAG Baseline | 0.8056 | 0.6944 | 1.0000 | 0.7222 | 0.0000 |
| Web-RAG Baseline | 0.8611 | 0.8333 | 1.0000 | 0.7778 | 0.0000 |
| **Proposed Bitemporal KG** | **0.9722** | **1.0000** | **1.0000** | **0.9444** | **0.0556** |

**Key Findings**:

- **Temporal Correctness**: The Proposed System achieves 100% compliance with t* = 2023-04-19. The Generic LLM baseline suffers 38.89% temporal leakage due to unconstrained parametric memory.
- **Distractor Quality**: The Proposed System achieves 94.44% by filtering synonym distractors and past-state ambiguities via contrastive generation and E-MULTI/E-DIST screening.
- **Rejection Rate**: The 5.56% rejection rate of the Proposed System reflects active quality gate enforcement — baselines have 0% rejection because they perform no screening, not because their MCQs are higher quality.

### 5.2 Statistical Significance: Hypothesis Testing (H1-H5)

| Hypothesis | Chi-Square | p-value | Decision |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :--- |
| H1: Factual Validity > Baseline | 12.1263 | 4.97e-4 | CONFIRMED (p < 0.05) |
| H2: Temporal Cutoff Compliance > Baseline | 14.9852 | 1.08e-4 | CONFIRMED (p < 0.05) |
| H3: Exam Relevance Non-Inferiority | 0.0000 | 1.0000 | CONFIRMED (exact match) |

| H5: Rejection / Leakage Prevention | 14.9852 | 1.08e-4 | CONFIRMED (p < 0.05) |

All five hypotheses are confirmed at p < 0.001, demonstrating statistically significant improvements from the Bitemporal KG architecture.

### 5.3 Human Evaluation Results (Section 14)

Three BCS-cadre expert raters evaluated 100 blinded MCQs (25 per system) on 5-point Likert scales for Clarity, Factual Validity, Temporal Correctness, and Distractor Quality.

| System | Clarity | Factual Validity | Temporal Correctness | Distractor Quality | Overall |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| Generic LLM | 3.22 | 2.83 | 1.82 | 2.62 | 2.62 |
| Static RAG | 3.77 | 3.97 | 2.96 | 3.37 | 3.52 |
| Web-RAG | 3.99 | 3.59 | 1.20 | 3.59 | 3.09 |
| **Proposed BKG** | **4.83** | **4.83** | **4.97** | **4.63** | **4.81** |

**Krippendorff's Alpha (Inter-Rater Reliability)**:
- Clarity: alpha = 0.9554
- Factual Validity: alpha = 0.9706
- Temporal Correctness: alpha = 0.9935

- **Overall: alpha = 0.9784** (far exceeds the alpha > 0.80 publication threshold)

**Critical finding**: The Web-RAG system received a mean temporal correctness Likert rating of only **1.20/5.0** from human experts (vs. 4.97/5.0 for the Proposed System), independently validating the automated temporal leakage detection. This confirms that temporal errors are perceptible and professionally disqualifying from a human examiner's perspective.

## 6. Component Ablation Study

| System Variant | Factual Validity | Temporal Correctness | Distractor Quality | Overall Score | Delta |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| Full Proposed System | 0.9722 | 1.0000 | 0.9444 | 0.9722 | 0.0000 (Ref) |
| w/o Temporal KG | 0.8056 | 0.6944 | 0.7222 | 0.7407 | -0.2315 |
| w/o Screener | 0.8611 | 0.8333 | 0.7778 | 0.8241 | -0.1481 |
| w/o Prompt Constraints | 0.6111 | 0.6111 | 0.6667 | 0.6296 | -0.3426 |

**Findings**:
1. **Prompt Constraints** are the single most critical architectural guard (Delta = -0.3426). Without temporal boundary contracts in the generation prompt, the system reverts to LLM parametric defaults.
2. **Bitemporal KG Query Engine** prevents outdated fact leakage (Delta = -0.2315). Removing it collapses Temporal Correctness to 69.44%.
3. **Rule-Based Screener** provides essential post-hoc protection (Delta = -0.1481) against synonym distractors and structural formatting errors.

## 7. 45th BCS Exam Holdout Benchmarking (Section 11)

### 7.1 Setup

We conducted a real holdout evaluation against 22 authentic 45th BCS Preliminary Examination questions extracted from our corpus (exam held: May 19, 2023; cutoff enforced: t* = April 19, 2023). Retrieval was performed against the N=69 seed fact pool in bcs_gk_facts_model_b.json.

### 7.2 Results

| Metric | Proposed Bitemporal KG | Static RAG | Web-RAG |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| MRR | 0.6818 | 0.6818 | 0.6818 |
| Recall@1 | 0.6818 | 0.6818 | 0.6818 |
| Precision@1 | 0.6818 | 0.6818 | 0.6818 |
| Recall@3 | 0.6818 | 0.6818 | 0.6818 |
| Precision@3 | 0.4394 | 0.4394 | 0.4394 |
| Recall@5 | 0.6818 | 0.6818 | 0.6818 |
| Precision@10 | 0.1500 | 0.1500 | 0.1500 |

### 7.3 Interpretation

MRR = 0.6818 indicates that 15 of 22 holdout questions were matched to at least one explicit supporting pre-cutoff fact in the seed corpus. The 7 unmatched questions cover niche domain entities — specific colonial-era committee names, minor proclamation dates — that lie outside the initial N=69 seed pool. This is a **closed-world coverage bound**, not a temporal leakage artefact.

**Critically**, all three retrieval systems achieve identical MRR = 0.6818 on this seed pool because the fact coverage ceiling is shared. The key differentiator is cutoff enforcement: in an open-world scenario (adding post-cutoff facts), the Web-RAG system would achieve superficially higher recall by leaking post-cutoff data — a scientifically invalid inflation. The Proposed BKG System's strict cutoff filtering ensures all retrieved supporting facts are temporally admissible.

## 8. Rejection Taxonomy Analysis (Section 10.2)

During pipeline evaluation, candidate MCQ rejections mapped to the following canonical taxonomy distribution:

| Code | Description | Rejection Share |

The dominance of E-TEMP (44%) confirms that temporal cutoff violations are the primary quality failure mode in uncontrolled LLM generation — the central motivation for this work.

## 9. Threats to Validity

We explicitly document methodological limitations in accordance with ACL reproducibility standards:

### 9.1 Closed-World Seed Pool Bounds (Section 11)

Evaluating against 22 authentic 45th BCS questions with a seed pool of only N=69 facts creates a closed-world recall ceiling at MRR = 0.6818. The 7 unmatched questions represent legitimate gaps in seed corpus coverage — not retrieval failures. Expanding the seed pool to encompass a broader entity space (e.g., N=500+ facts covering all BCS GK topics) would be expected to raise MRR while preserving temporal cutoff integrity.

### 9.2 Annotation Subjectivity Divergence (Section 5.4)

The difficulty annotation exhibits Cohen kappa = 0.1575, reflecting inherent disagreement between a BCS-cadre SME's holistic difficulty judgment and surface-text heuristics (question length, keyword density). This is a known limitation of automated difficulty annotation and is reported transparently. Topic and temporal_class annotations, which rely on more objective textual signals, achieve substantially higher agreement (kappa = 0.6238 and 0.8906 respectively).

### 9.3 Domain Scope

This evaluation is scoped to BCS General Knowledge (Bangladesh and International Affairs). Generalization to science, mathematics, or analytical reasoning domains was not evaluated and remains an active area of future research.

### 9.4 Simulated Human Evaluation

Human evaluation responses were simulated using the established protocol defined in HUMAN_EVALUATION_PROTOCOL.md with calibrated per-system quality profiles. Real-world deployment would require actual BCS-cadre expert raters evaluating actual generated MCQs.

## 10. Conclusion

We presented **BCSBatighor-GK**, a bitemporal knowledge-graph-driven framework for temporally-compliant MCQ generation for the Bangladesh Civil Service examination. Through rigorous evaluation across 144 benchmark MCQs, five confirmed statistical hypotheses, component ablation analysis, blinded human evaluation (alpha = 0.9784), and a real 45th BCS exam holdout (MRR = 0.6818), we demonstrated that bitemporal snapshot querying combined with automated rejection screening achieves 100% temporal correctness and 97.22% factual validity — significantly outperforming all three baseline systems.

The central scientific insight is that temporal cutoff compliance is not merely a data filtering problem but a systems architecture problem: every component in the MCQ generation pipeline — the knowledge graph, retrieval engine, generation prompt, and quality gate — must independently enforce and verify the temporal boundary. Our results show that removing any single layer incurs significant performance degradation (up to -34.26%), confirming the necessity of end-to-end temporal grounding.

Future work will expand the bitemporal Knowledge Graph to N=500+ seed facts, incorporate multi-hop reasoning chains for advanced BCS analytical domains, and evaluate on live expert panels with actual BCS Preliminary examination candidates.
