[P30] [Heading 1] 1. Research framing and non-negotiable contribution claims
[P31] [Normal] The paper should not be presented as “we scraped BCS questions and used an agent to generate more questions.” That framing is too application-centric and invites the reviewer response that the method is merely RAG plus prompting. The scientific framing must be temporally grounded question generation in a non-stationary knowledge domain, with BCS-GK as a real-world, low-resource, high-stakes testbed.
[P32] [Heading 2] 1.1 Recommended central problem statement
[P33] [Normal] Q_t = G(z, KG_t, P_z, S_BCS)
[P34] [Normal] Here z is a selected topic, KG_t is the reconstructed temporal knowledge-graph snapshot valid at target time t, P_z is the historical pattern of BCS questions for topic z, and S_BCS represents BCS-style constraints such as expected scope, option format, difficulty, and language style.
[P35] [Heading 2] 1.2 Contribution stack to aim for
[P37] [Heading 2] 1.3 Claims the paper should avoid unless the evidence supports them
[P38] [List Bullet] “First temporal question-generation system” — too broad; temporal QA and temporal KG work is extensive. Claim the specific intersection you actually establish.
[P39] [List Bullet] “Predicts BCS exam questions” — use careful wording such as forecasts / generates candidate MCQs and measures coverage of held-out exam facts. Exact wording prediction is neither necessary nor scientifically desirable.
[P40] [List Bullet] “Dynamic KG guarantees truth” — the graph is only as reliable as sources, extraction, temporal normalization, and conflict resolution. Report graph quality explicitly.
[P41] [List Bullet] “LLM-as-judge replaces humans” — use judge models as scalable auxiliary evaluators, then establish agreement with human BCS-domain raters.
[P42] [Heading 1] 2. Research questions and hypotheses
[P44] [Heading 2] 2.1 Testable hypotheses
[P45] [List Bullet] H1: Full temporal-KG generation will yield higher Temporal Validity Rate and lower Post-Cutoff Leakage Rate than all unstructured retrieval baselines.
[P46] [List Bullet] H2: The temporal KG will produce larger gains on mutable/dynamic questions than on evergreen/static questions.
[P47] [List Bullet] H3: Topic-guided acquisition will improve evidence precision and reduce crawl/retrieval cost relative to unrestricted web retrieval without materially reducing held-out 2023 topic/fact recall.
[P48] [List Bullet] H4: Temporal distractors sourced from type-compatible historical graph states will increase human-rated plausibility while preserving Single-Correct-Option Rate.
[P49] [List Bullet] H5: Removing the temporal verifier or provenance checks will disproportionately increase subtle temporal errors even when general factuality metrics remain high.

[P51] [Normal] For the main experiment, treat the target as the 45th BCS preliminary examination held on 19 May 2023. Contemporary reporting stated that the Bangladesh Public Service Commission set the preliminary MCQ exam for that date. If the project ultimately targets a different 2023 BCS paper, replace t* everywhere while preserving the protocol.

[P54] [Heading 2] 3.1 The data firewall
[P56] [Heading 2] 3.2 Cutoff choices: report more than one
[P57] [Normal] Because examination questions are usually prepared before the exam date, using knowledge available at 09:59 on exam day is generous. The primary analysis should therefore define one main cutoff and perform sensitivity analysis with stricter cutoffs.
[P59] [Heading 2] 3.3 Preventing hidden temporal contamination
[P60] [List Bullet] Do not use search-result snippets without recording the page’s original publication date and any “updated” date.
[P61] [List Bullet] Reject pages created after the cutoff even if they describe earlier events; otherwise the system benefits from hindsight.
[P62] [List Bullet] For pages that existed before the cutoff but were updated later, use an archived snapshot or another contemporaneous source for the relevant claim.
[P63] [List Bullet] Do not let the LLM browse freely during the main evaluation. Retrieval must pass through a cutoff-enforcing tool that logs every source and date.
[P64] [List Bullet] Freeze prompts, source-policy rules, ranker weights, and all model choices before inspecting target-set performance.
[P65] [List Bullet] Compute near-duplicate similarity between generated questions and the hidden target text after generation. Unexpectedly high lexical overlap should trigger a leakage investigation.

[P87] [Heading 1] 5. Topic ontology and topic-grounded knowledge demand
[P88] [Normal] The historical corpus should determine what knowledge the system maintains. This is stronger than unrestricted crawling because it provides an explicit information-demand model: recurring BCS topics define a bounded, auditable set of entity types and relations that the temporal KG should track.
[P89] [Heading 2] 5.1 Build a hierarchical ontology
[P91] [Heading 2] 5.2 Topic discovery recipe
[P92] [List Number] Encode historical questions using a multilingual sentence embedding model.
[P93] [List Number] Cluster within top-level BCS categories, not globally. This prevents unrelated high-frequency language from dominating clusters.
[P94] [List Number] Ask an LLM to propose short cluster labels and candidate parent/child relations.
[P95] [List Number] Have an RA merge synonyms, split over-broad clusters, and map each topic to an approved label.
[P96] [List Number] For each topic, extract recurrent entity types and relation templates from the questions.
[P97] [List Number] Create a topic-demand profile: frequency, recency, temporal mutability, historical recurrence, and source types required for updates.
[P98] [Heading 2] 5.3 Exam-likelihood score for ranking generated candidates
[P99] [Normal] If the 2023 experiment asks the system to produce a ranked candidate pool, define an exam-likelihood score using only pre-target information. Do not fit its weights on the 2023 target set.
[P100] [Normal] Score(q) = w1·TopicPrior + w2·Recency + w3·TemporalSalience + w4·HistoricalRecurrence + w5·SourceConfidence − w6·Redundancy
[P101] [Normal] Tune the weights on an earlier pseudo-holdout (for example, use exams up to year y−1 to forecast year y). Then lock the weights before the 2023 run.
[P102] [Heading 1] 6. Dynamic bitemporal knowledge graph
[P104] [Small Note] Figure 2. Recommended end-to-end architecture.
[P105] [Heading 2] 6.1 Why a bitemporal graph rather than a latest-value database
[P106] [Normal] Never overwrite a changed fact. The system must preserve historical states so that it can reconstruct what was true at an arbitrary cutoff. Store both valid time (when the fact is true in the world) and observation/ingestion time (when the system learned it).
[P107] [Normal] f = (s, r, o, [t_valid_start, t_valid_end), [t_obs_start, t_obs_end), provenance, confidence)
[P108] [Normal] Example conceptually: if Person A held Office X until June 2022 and Person B held it thereafter, both statements remain in the graph with non-overlapping validity intervals. A query for May 2022 should return A; a query for May 2023 should return B.
[P109] [Heading 2] 6.2 Minimum graph objects
[P111] [Heading 2] 6.3 Recommended claim schema

[P115] [Code Block] object_id_or_value: entity or literal

[P124] [Heading 2] 6.4 Entity linking and relation normalization
[P125] [List Bullet] Canonicalize Bangla and English surface forms to the same entity where appropriate; retain aliases in both scripts.
[P126] [List Bullet] Normalize titles separately from people (e.g., the office is an entity; the office-holder relation is temporal).
[P127] [List Bullet] Use a controlled relation vocabulary. A small, high-precision relation set is better than hundreds of inconsistent predicates.
[P128] [List Bullet] Log an entity-link confidence score and route uncertain cases to human review.
[P129] [List Bullet] For numeric values, store unit, period, reporting agency, and revision status. Numbers without time and unit are unsafe for GK generation.
[P130] [Heading 2] 6.5 Temporal normalization rules
[P131] [List Bullet] Prefer explicit date intervals from primary evidence.
[P132] [List Bullet] If only a start date is known, keep valid_to open until a superseding claim is verified.
[P133] [List Bullet] If the source says “as of 2022,” represent the granularity honestly; do not invent a day/month.
[P134] [List Bullet] Distinguish event time from publication time. An event on 10 January reported on 12 January has valid_from=10 Jan but source_published_at=12 Jan.
[P135] [List Bullet] Distinguish retrospective reports from contemporaneous evidence. Retrospective pages can help audit truth but must not enter a pre-cutoff generation run if published after the cutoff.

[P179] [Heading 1] 9. Temporally contrastive distractor generation
[P180] [Normal] Distractors are not an afterthought. Temporal GK questions offer a unique opportunity: a historically correct value can be a highly plausible but currently incorrect distractor. The graph lets you construct these systematically while still verifying that only one option is correct at t*.
[P181] [Normal] D_t = { o_j : type(o_j)=type(o*), o_j ≠ o*, and o_j is not correct for (s,r,?,t) }
[P182] [Heading 2] 9.1 Distractor sources in preferred order
[P183] [List Number] Temporal predecessor/successor for the same subject–relation (e.g., previous office holder).
[P184] [List Number] Same-type entities linked to a sibling relation or related institution.
[P185] [List Number] Near-neighbor values from the same topic, ranking, event, or organization.
[P186] [List Number] Historically confused entities observed in prior BCS options.
[P187] [List Number] LLM-generated candidates only when graph alternatives are unavailable; these require stricter verification.
[P188] [Heading 2] 9.2 Hard constraints
[P189] [List Bullet] Exactly one correct option under the target temporal snapshot.
[P190] [List Bullet] All distractors must match the answer type and grammatical form.
[P191] [List Bullet] No distractor may be a synonym/alias of the correct answer.
[P192] [List Bullet] No distractor may become correct due to underspecified time in the stem.
[P193] [List Bullet] A distractor can be historically true only if the stem clearly anchors the target time or the exam-time interpretation makes it unambiguous.
[P194] [List Bullet] Avoid absurd distractors: high plausibility is desired, but ambiguity is not.

[P210] [Normal] This is the experiment that should carry the paper. The objective is not to regenerate the exact wording of the held-out paper. [P243] [Heading 3] Why report both N=50 and K=200?
[P244] [Normal] N=50 answers the realistic question: can the system create one exam-sized paper whose content resembles the held-out exam? K=200 answers a forecasting question: does the system place the actual examined facts within a broader candidate pool? Reviewers will understand that exact examination selection is partly stochastic and editorial, so Recall@100/200 provides a fairer measure of knowledge/topic anticipation while Precision@50 measures focus.

[P286] [Heading 1] 17. Novelty positioning against recent ACL / EMNLP / NAACL work
[P287] [Normal] The literature should be organized by research gap, not by a chronological list. The closest works establish that (i) LLM/KB methods can generate questions, (ii) temporally changing knowledge causes QA failures and requires temporal retrieval/graphs, and (iii) agents can reason over temporal KGs. The paper’s differentiator is using a topic-conditioned, dynamically maintained temporal KG as the knowledge substrate for MCQ generation and evaluating the generator through a genuine historical exam holdout.
[P289] [Heading 2] 17.1 The novelty sentence to defend
[P290] [Normal] Before final submission, repeat the literature search and weaken “to our knowledge” if a directly overlapping paper appears. Since this is a rapidly moving area, novelty should be stated as the combination of capabilities and evaluation protocol, not as a brittle “first-ever temporal QG” claim.
[P291] [Heading 2] 17.2 How to cite the recent literature in Related Work
[P292] [List Bullet] Paragraph A — Question generation: KQG-CoT and MCQG-SRefine; explain why factual freshness is not solved by self-refinement alone.
[P293] [List Bullet] Paragraph B — Dynamic/evolving knowledge: TemporalWiki, DYNAMICQA, EvoWiki, EverGreenQA; motivate mutable vs evergreen fact classes and the need for snapshots.
[P294] [List Bullet] Paragraph C — Time-sensitive retrieval/QA: temporal graph fusion and MRAG; motivate explicit temporal relevance and structured evidence.

[P296] [List Bullet] Paragraph E — Bangladesh/low-resource agentic systems: Mina as an important local precedent but opposite task direction (answering existing exam questions vs generating future/as-of-time exam questions).

[P334] [List Bullet] Respect robots.txt, site terms, rate limits, and copyright. The paper should emphasize factual extraction and provenance, not mass redistribution of third-party webpages.
[P335] [List Bullet] Do not treat a single news article as ground truth for sensitive or contested current-affairs claims when authoritative sources exist.
[P336] [List Bullet] Preserve uncertainty and conflict rather than forcing every claim to one value.
[P337] [List Bullet] Avoid claims that the system can replace official exam setters or predict confidential future questions. Frame it as temporally grounded assessment generation and historical forecasting evaluation.
[P338] [List Bullet] Clearly distinguish actual BCS questions from generated mock questions in any demo or release.
